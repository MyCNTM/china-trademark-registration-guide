## Start with what your business actually sells

1. **Search your products or services** in the [online classification tool](https://china-trademark.net/guide/classification/search.html). For example, search `clothing`, `software` or `上衣`.
2. **Build and export a working list.** Add suitable items, review their classes and groups, then copy the list or download CSV. The tool also estimates our standard filing package for one trademark.
3. **Check the trademark itself before filing.** A goods catalogue identifies filing terms; it does not tell you whether your brand conflicts with earlier trademarks. [Visit MY CNTM](https://china-trademark.net/) for a preliminary search and help reviewing your specification.

In China, the class number alone is not enough. Similar-group notes, cross-references and the actual goods wording can affect the analysis. The [classification guide](https://china-trademark.net/guide/guides/05-nice-classification-guide-china.html) explains how to use these details without assuming that one selected item protects an entire class.

## Need help registering your trademark in China?

[MY CNTM](https://china-trademark.net/) helps foreign brand owners with trademark searches, Chinese goods specifications, applications and follow-up, with English-language communication.

Our standard direct-filing package is **USD 199 per trademark, per class**, including the official filing fee and our service fee for **up to ten goods/services items**. Each additional item in the same class is **USD 10**. Refusal responses, oppositions and other additional proceedings are quoted separately.

Search first, then review the proposed specification and written quote before paying. A filing does not guarantee registration.

**Snapshot:** `cnipa-20261019`, synchronised with the client portal on **18 November 2026**. The synchronisation date is not the effective date of a new Nice edition. English translations support review; confirm the current Chinese wording and applicable classification notes before filing.

Item codes are retained where supplied, but some acceptable names have no code. Do not use the item code alone as a unique database key. See the [dataset documentation](classification/README.md) for fields and examples.
